\section{Conclusion}

This paper asked a deliberately narrow question: under a matched measurement budget, can lightweight evidence — compile-adjacent signals and a small amount of runtime profiling — guide Triton kernel tuning to better schedules than default settings or equally budgeted random search? The answer is yes, but only in a constrained and carefully evidenced sense.

The strongest contribution of this work is not a large final speedup. It is a clearer account of what kinds of evidence remain useful once the budget is fixed and the workloads are representative. Cheap compile-adjacent signals prune better than they rank. Convenient workloads can make a selector look stronger than it really is on realistic shapes. Runtime profiling helps in some kernels and regimes and not in others. Transfer failures are informative rather than embarrassing, because they justify retiring unstable knob families rather than stretching the evaluation around them.

Within that disciplined framing, the final conservative selector delivers a small but credible improvement on representative matrix multiplication: a geometric-mean speedup of $1.03\times$ over default settings, compared with $0.81\times$ for the earlier compile-signal-ranked parent and $1.00\times$ for naive random search under the same matched budget. Triton tuning is not solved here. But matched-budget, evidence-aware tuning does yield interpretable and practically useful guidance when the evidence hierarchy and the workload program are designed carefully from the start.
