\section{The \system Framework}
\label{sec:design}

\system is a tuning harness built around the evidence hierarchy described in the previous section. Rather than introducing a new search algorithm, it fixes the rules of the experiment so that every comparison in this paper operates under the same measurement budget and consults the same ladder of increasingly informative evidence. The framework has five ingredients, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}: candidate generation with explicit admissibility bounds, a ladder of selectors that differ only in the evidence they consult, a distinction between reportable and diagnostic evidence, a narrow final evaluation surface, and a deliberately conservative final selector.

\subsection{Candidate generation and admissibility}

For each kernel, we enumerate the Cartesian product of the tuning knobs that are exposed to the developer. Every candidate is then checked against explicit admissibility limits: configurations that would exceed the available shared memory, or that would launch with invalid tile shapes for a given input, are rejected up front. These bounds are made explicit in the framework rather than being silently applied inside the generator, because hidden truncation would turn a selector study into an artifact of candidate ordering rather than a real comparison.

\subsection{A ladder of selectors}

We compare a fixed ladder of increasingly informed selectors, each operating under the same matched budget. The ladder includes the kernel's default configuration, a naive random search, a grid baseline, a selector that ranks candidates using compile-adjacent signals only, a variant that additionally consults a small amount of runtime profiling, and a handful of revised selectors that adjust how the compile-adjacent frontier is constructed in response to the empirical findings of the study. The ladder is part of the scientific design: every claimed improvement in the paper has to beat a comparable matched-budget baseline, not merely the kernel default.

\subsection{Reportable and diagnostic evidence}

Not all evidence we collect is treated as equally load-bearing. We distinguish \emph{reportable} evidence, which supports the main claims of the paper, from \emph{diagnostic} evidence, which is used to explain outcomes without being counted as superiority evidence. Compile-adjacent signals, and a small, stably-attributed set of profiling counters, fall into the reportable tier. Additional counters may be collected when we need to understand why a particular selector failed, but they are used only for failure analysis. This separation is what allows us to present a negative result — such as the failure of one reduction-splitting knob family to generalize across search spaces — without quietly reusing the diagnostic counters to argue for the main result.

\subsection{The final evaluation surface}

The schedule space evaluated by the final paper is narrower than the space we began with. For matrix multiplication, the final tuning knobs are the three tile dimensions of the output block, a grouping factor that controls thread-block launch order, the number of warps per block, and the number of pipeline stages. We explicitly remove a reduction-splitting parameter that was evaluated in an earlier expanded space; the reasons for this removal are the subject of Section~\ref{sec:results}. For layer normalization, the final knobs are the block size, the number of warps, and the number of pipeline stages; a row-grouping parameter is likewise removed from the final surface after failing to improve matched-budget comparisons. These exclusions are not bookkeeping decisions. They are direct consequences of the transfer and micro-study evidence discussed in the next two sections.

\subsection{A deliberately conservative final selector}

The last revision of our selector is intentionally narrow. Rather than introducing another broad heuristic, it corrects how the compile-adjacent frontier is constructed on the remaining matrix multiplication surface, so that strong compile-ranked families are preserved and balanced candidates that earlier revisions had missed are re-admitted into the evaluation set. As we show in Section~\ref{sec:results}, most of the final improvement is already present before runtime profiling is applied; profiling contributes only a small additional increment on top of the already-corrected frontier.
