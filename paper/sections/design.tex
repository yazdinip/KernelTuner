\section{\system Design}
\label{sec:design}

\system is built around an evidence hierarchy rather than around an open-ended search heuristic. The selector sees only evidence types that the paper is willing to defend: admissibility checks, cheap compile-adjacent signals, bounded reportable profiling, and held-out runtime evaluation.

\paragraph{Candidate generation and admissibility.}
The generator enumerates the declared schedule space, validates kernel-specific configurations, and enforces admissibility limits explicitly rather than silently truncating the raw Cartesian product. This matters because hidden truncation would turn a selector study into a config-ordering artifact.

\paragraph{Selector ladder.}
The framework uses a fixed ladder of comparators: \texttt{default\_config}, naive random and grid baselines, compile-ranked selection, bounded profiling, and revised selectors under the same budget. This ladder is part of the scientific design. Every claimed improvement must beat a matched-budget parent rather than a weaker or cheaper baseline.

\paragraph{Signal tiers.}
Tier 0 signals are broad and cheap. Tier 1 signals are bounded and reportable only when their attribution is stable enough to defend. Diagnostic counters are allowed to explain failures, but they are not used as direct superiority evidence. That rule is what allows the paper to use schedule diagnostics to explain the failure of \texttt{split\_k} without quietly folding those diagnostics into the main tuning contract.

\paragraph{Final mainline surfaces.}
The final reportable surfaces are narrower than the exploratory ones. GEMM keeps \texttt{block\_m}, \texttt{block\_n}, \texttt{block\_k}, \texttt{group\_size\_m}, \texttt{num\_warps}, and \texttt{num\_stages}; \texttt{split\_k} is retired to diagnostic status. LayerNorm keeps \texttt{block\_size}, \texttt{num\_warps}, and \texttt{num\_stages}; \texttt{rows\_per\_program} is likewise retired to diagnostic status. These boundaries are not bookkeeping. They are the practical outcome of the transfer and microstudy evidence.

\paragraph{Final guarded selector family.}
The last selector change is deliberately conservative. Instead of opening another broad revision family, the final pass corrects frontier construction on the non-\texttt{split\_k} GEMM surface by preserving strong compile-ranked families while recovering balanced candidates that earlier revisions had missed. The final ablation shows that once the frontier is corrected, most of the gain is already present before profiling, and profiling adds only a small extra increment.
