\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The project's most general lesson is that matched-budget tuning is less about finding any fast configuration than about deciding which evidence is worth paying for. Compile-adjacent signals are clearly useful, but mostly as pruning evidence. Once the candidate set narrows to several plausible GEMM families, those same signals are too weak to solve ranking on their own. That observation is more broadly useful than the final headline number because it explains where lightweight tuning should be trusted and where it should not.

The second lesson is that workload design changes the apparent answer. Aligned GEMM remains in the paper not because it is the main truth source, but because it demonstrates how an easier workload can flatter a selector. The paper's strongest methodological claim is therefore not just that the final selector is better than its parent on one study; it is that representative workloads are necessary to make that comparison scientifically meaningful.

The third lesson is that bounded negative results improve the final story. LayerNorm stays secondary because the evidence never justifies a strong matched-budget success claim, and the expanded \texttt{split\_k} space stays negative because the transfer-safe corrective pass does not generalize. Those outcomes are not detours around the main contribution. They are why the final non-\texttt{split\_k} mainline result can be presented credibly rather than as the survivor of uncontrolled heuristic growth.

What is settled now is intentionally narrower than what the broader autotuning literature often targets. The paper establishes that a fixed-kernel Triton tuning study can produce interpretable lessons about pruning, ranking, workload realism, profiling, and transfer under explicit budgets. It does not establish a universal Triton autotuner, a multi-architecture result, or a substitute for richer compiler-level schedule synthesis.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single architecture.} All promoted primary studies use one homogeneous RTX A6000 pool. The paper makes no multi-architecture claim.
\item \textbf{Fixed kernels.} The study keeps the Triton kernels fixed. It does not compare against broader algorithmic alternatives or full-kernel synthesis.
\item \textbf{Bounded configuration spaces.} Every reported result comes from explicitly bounded schedule spaces. This improves fairness and interpretability, but it limits generality.
\item \textbf{Limited kernel coverage.} Representative GEMM is the truth source and LayerNorm is secondary. The paper does not claim that the same lessons hold for all Triton kernels.
\item \textbf{Course-scale empirical scope.} The contribution is a careful matched-budget systems study, not a universal autotuning benchmark or production-compiler evaluation.
\end{itemize}

These limitations are part of the paper's argument rather than an afterthought. The value of the study is that it states exactly where the method works, where it fails, and why the final positive claim remains bounded.
