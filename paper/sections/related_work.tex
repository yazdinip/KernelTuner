\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Our work sits between broad autoscheduling systems and purely local benchmark-first autotuning. The closest neighbor depends on which level of abstraction one emphasizes, so the important distinction is not whether prior work studies schedule search at all, but which search surface and which evidence contract it assumes.

\paragraph{Autoscheduling and schedule search.}
The broadest conceptual lineage comes from Halide, which made schedule choice an explicit optimization object rather than an implementation detail entangled with correctness \citep{ragankelley2012halide}. Subsequent Halide autoschedulers showed that schedule spaces could be searched automatically, including with learned guidance and structured exploration \citep{mullapudi2016halide,adams2019halide}. This literature establishes the core premise behind our study: schedule choice can dominate performance even when the high-level computation is fixed. Our setting is narrower because we do not search a rich transformation language; we study a bounded Triton meta-parameter space whose knobs already have direct operational meaning for kernel authors.

\paragraph{Tensor-compiler autotuning and learned cost models.}
TVM, AutoTVM, Ansor, TensorIR, and MetaSchedule push schedule search into richer compiler and IR-level optimization settings \citep{chen2018tvm,chen2018learningtensor,zheng2020ansor,shao2022metaschedule,zheng2022tensorir}. These systems are scientifically adjacent because they also ask how structured evidence can guide performance search. The difference is scope. Their objective is broader compiler optimization over richer transformation spaces. Our objective is to understand what limited evidence can and cannot buy on a fixed-kernel Triton surface under an explicit matched budget.

\paragraph{Triton-native tuning.}
Triton is the immediate ecosystem context for this paper. Its language and compiler make schedule-oriented GPU programming practical while still exposing tile sizes, warp counts, and pipeline stages as performance-critical parameters \citep{tillet2019triton,openai2021triton}. Triton also includes built-in hooks such as \texttt{triton.autotune} and \texttt{triton.heuristics} \citep{tritondocsautotune,tritondocsheuristics}, and PyTorch increasingly treats Triton as part of a larger compilation and autotuning stack \citep{pytorchcompile2026,pytorchtriton2026}. This ecosystem matters because it shows that bounded tuning is already operationally relevant. Our contribution is not another autotune API. It is an empirical account of what lightweight evidence buys before broad benchmark-driven search takes over.

\paragraph{External kernel autotuners.}
Methodologically, \system is closest to external autotuning frameworks such as OpenTuner, Kernel Tuner, CLTune, and KTT \citep{ansel2014opentuner,vanwerkhoven2019kerneltuner,nugteren2015cltune,petrov2023ktt}. These systems treat kernels as parameterized programs, generate candidate configurations, validate correctness, benchmark performance, and compare search strategies. Our work follows that practical lineage more closely than it follows compiler-level autoscheduling. The main difference is the evidence contract: \system specializes to Triton, enforces matched-budget comparisons, separates reportable profiling from diagnostic profiling, and treats failure analysis as part of the contribution rather than as leftover benchmark commentary.

\paragraph{Recent agentic GPU optimization.}
Recent systems such as CUDA Agent treat GPU optimization as a broader synthesis problem over full CUDA kernels \citep{dai2026cudaagent}. This is important context because it shows how quickly the automation frontier has moved. It also clarifies our niche: we are not competing on full-kernel generation. We keep the Triton implementation fixed and ask a stricter question: given a bounded schedule space and a fixed evidence budget, when do cheap signals help, when does profiling help, and when should a knob family be retired entirely? That narrower scope is what makes the negative results in this paper interpretable rather than merely disappointing.

\paragraph{Practical ecosystem motivation.}
The wider Triton ecosystem suggests that tuning overhead remains a real engineering problem even when native autotuning support exists. Official tutorials present tuning as standard practice for performance-critical kernels, and tools such as \texttt{triton-dejavu} target the reuse and overhead problems around repeated autotuning \citep{ibm2025dejavu}. We use that ecosystem as motivation rather than as a source of superiority claims. It helps explain why a lightweight, reproducible, matched-budget tuning study is still worth doing.

Taken together, the literature supports a clear middle-ground contribution. This paper is narrower than compiler-scale autoscheduling and narrower than agentic full-kernel optimization, but it is more interpretable as a controlled empirical systems study of what lightweight evidence can and cannot buy on Triton schedule surfaces.
